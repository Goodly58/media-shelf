03 How it works

Gathered, scored, then checked

Three passes stand between a public listing and a card on these shelves.

1
1

Gather

Titles are collected from public sources across every genre, era and niche — blockbusters and deep cuts alike.

2
2

Score

Each entry picks up its critic rating: Metacritic and IGN for games, Metacritic, IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes for film and TV, Goodreads ratings and vote counts for books.

3
3

Verify & merge

Top scores are re-checked against Metacritic and earn a ✓. Duplicate entries are merged rather than stacked.

05 The fine print

Honest about the data

What is checked, what is approximate, and what earns a place on a shelf.

These catalogues were compiled with AI assistance from public sources. Scores marked ✓ were checked against Metacritic directly. Everything else — IGN numbers, Goodreads ratings, release years, blurbs — is a close approximation and may be wrong in the details. Shelf is a discovery tool, not a citation source. Cover art belongs to its respective publishers.

New releases are added automatically on the 2nd of every month — last refreshed —.

What gets included

This is a curated shelf, not an exhaustive database, so a title has to have been noticed by the wider world to appear. That is a popularity bar, not a quality one — poorly reviewed titles are welcome once they clear it.

  • Games — released on PC, and either carry a Metacritic score or have 1,000+ Steam reviews
  • Films — a Metacritic score, 25+ Rotten Tomatoes critics, or 5,000+ IMDb votes
  • Series — at least one season aired, plus a Metacritic score or 5,000+ IMDb votes
  • Books — 1,000+ Goodreads ratings, a major prize listing, or a bestseller-list appearance
